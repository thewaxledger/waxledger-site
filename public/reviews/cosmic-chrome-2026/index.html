2026 Topps Cosmic Chrome Baseball

5.2WAX SCORE / 10
  • Hobby: 20 packs of 4 (80 cards), 8 boxes a case. Topps presale $489.99 on Oct 5, up from $329.99 last year. eBay presale sold: $900 median on 76 single boxes Oct 5 to 23 ($700 to $1,000), $950 median on 8 boxes Oct 27 to 31
  • Fewer than one autograph a box: Cardsmiths Breaks' per-box averages put it at about one in 2.7 hobby boxes; Checklist Insider's pack odds (1:136 for the 58-card main set, 1:653 and longer for the other five) work out closer to one in five. The box sells on the colour parallels, not the hit
  • 200-card base set with 77 rookies: Roman Anthony #122, Konnor Griffin #24, Kevin McGonigle #132, Munetaka Murakami #21, Jac Caglianone #5, Jacob Misiorowski #148, Chase Burns #184, Travis Bazzana #27, Tatsuya Imai #75, Kazuma Okamoto #130, Trey Yesavage #71, Cam Schlittler #66, Andrew Painter #177, Bryce Eldridge #73, Jonah Tong #6, Samuel Basallo #30. Nick Kurtz #136 is in the set without the RC mark
  • Per hobby box (Cardsmiths Breaks' averages): about 62 base, 2 Refractors, 1 Nucleus, a Magenta Cosmos /299 in four of five boxes, Aqua /199 and White Hole in one of two, Purple /150 in one of 2.5, Blue Moon /99 in one of 3.8, Green /75 in one of five, Gold /50 about one a case, Orange /25 one in 15 boxes, Black /10 one in five cases, Red Flare /5 one in nine cases, SuperFractor one in 47 cases

Written Nov 2, two days before the hobby release (Nov 4). Topps sold the presale on Oct 5 at $489.99, $160 more than last year's $329.99, and eBay has priced it well above that: 76 single presale boxes sold for a $900 median between Oct 5 and 23, and the eight that sold in the last week of October went for $950. No 2026 single has a completed sale yet, so every single-card price below is pending and will be filled in once cases are open; the structure and last year's product are enough for a first verdict. Cosmic Chrome is a parallel product. A box is 80 cards and a hit is the exception (one autograph in roughly three to five boxes, depending on whose odds you read), so what you are buying for $900 to $950 is about two numbered colour refractors (Cardsmiths Breaks' averages, Magenta /299 through Orange /25) and the chance that one of them is an Anthony, Griffin, McGonigle or Murakami in a short colour. The rookie class is the strongest Topps has had in years and the design is the one that holds its value on the secondary market (2025 boxes sell for $1,150 a year on), which is the case for it. The case against is the price: at $950 a box, a case is $7,600 for about two or three autographs, one Gold /50 and roughly 19 other numbered cards.

Tier 1 · The cards worth opening a hobby box for

  • Roman Anthony #122 RC · colour refractors: Orange /25, Black Eclipse /10, Red Flare /5, SuperFractor 1/1Prices pending. Comp: his 2026 Topps #189 base RC is $40 in PSA 10 on 158 sales in 90 days (Raw-to-Gem board, Nov 1); 2025 Cosmic Chrome Sasaki base RC about $4 on 27 sales

    The rookie of the set and the card every Cosmic Chrome break is sold on. The base and Refractor will be cheap (last year's Sasaki base is $4); the numbered colours under /99 are the ones with a market, and the Black /10 and Red /5 are case-level hits that come out of one box in five to nine cases.

  • Konnor Griffin #24, Kevin McGonigle #132, Munetaka Murakami #21 · numbered coloursPrices pending. Comps: Griffin 2024 Bowman Draft Chrome 1st PSA 10 $134, McGonigle 2025 Bowman Chrome 1st PSA 10 $140, Murakami 2026 Bowman Chrome RC PSA 10 $220 (Raw-to-Gem board, Oct 11 to Nov 1)

    The three names after Anthony. Murakami is the one with the most open money behind him (his Bowman paper RC trades 167 times a month raw) and the one whose first Chrome-brand MLB card this is; Griffin and McGonigle are prospect money moving to a rookie card.

  • Autographs · Cosmic Chrome Autographs (58 cards, 1:136), Extraterrestrial Talent (20, 1:653), Launched Into Orbit (32, 1:1,051), Stellar Stars (21, 1:1,622), Star Clusters duals (20, 1:15,535); parallels Green /75 to SuperFractor 1/1Prices pending

    Not in every box, and the odds say most boxes have none: Cardsmiths' averages put an autograph in one of 2.7 boxes, Checklist Insider's pack odds nearer one in five. When one comes it is usually from the 58-card main set, which is rookies and second-year players on sticker; the dual and the Stellar Stars are the ones worth something.

  • Cosmic Explorers Cut Signatures · 17 cards, 1:85,901 packs: Einstein, Sagan, Armstrong, Sally Ride, NewtonPrices pending. No sale possible yet

    New this year and the one card in the set that is not a baseball card. At 1:85,901 packs that is one in about 537 cases; it is a lottery ticket, not a reason to buy.

Tier 2 · Mid-range: the numbered middle

  • Numbered base refractors · Magenta Cosmos /299 (4 in 5 boxes), Aqua Equinox /199 (1 in 2), Purple Nebula /150 (1 in 2.5), Blue Moon /99 (1 in 3.8), Green Space Dust /75 (1 in 5), Gold Interstellar /50 (about 1 a case), Orange Galactic /25 (1 in 15 boxes)Prices pending

    This is the product. A box returns about two numbered cards on average, and which player is on them decides whether the box paid: a Magenta /299 of a veteran is a $5 card, a Gold /50 Anthony is not. The new Magenta /299 adds a numbered card to most boxes without adding value.

  • Planetary Pursuit · 100 cards in ten planet tiers, Sun 1:120 packs to Pluto 1:28,000; planet number now on the frontPrices pending

    The insert that gives Cosmic Chrome its case-hit ladder. The rarer planets (Neptune, Pluto) of Anthony or Ohtani will be the biggest non-autograph sales of the release; the Sun and Mercury tiers are $5 to $20 cards in a normal year.

  • Variations · Constellation (25 cards, 1:605) and Anti-Gravity (25 cards, 1:961; Black /10, Red /5, SuperFractor 1/1)Prices pending

    One Constellation variation in about 30 boxes and one Anti-Gravity in 48. Both are new designs; last year's variations sold on the player, not the design.

  • StarFractor (100 cards, 1:282), Cosmic Dust (25, 1:606), Planetarium (25, 1:961), Mars Attacks MLB (10, 1:1,513)Prices pending

    Rare inserts with one or two chase names each. Mars Attacks is the collaboration with Topps' own sci-fi brand and the one that will trade on novelty; StarFractor is the pattern card that holds up.

Tier 3 · Everything else in the box

  • Base cards · about 62 of the 80, plus 2 Refractors (1:10) and a Nucleus Refractor (1:20) per boxPrices pending. Comp: 2025 Cosmic Chrome Sasaki base RC about $4 on 27 sales

    Three quarters of the box. The base card is a chrome design with a dark background and sells for a dollar or two outside the top four rookies; the unnumbered Refractor and Nucleus add a little.

  • Common inserts · Extraterrestrial Talent (35 cards, 1:6), Forces of the Diamond (22, 1:8), Dual Star Clusters (8, 1:25), Launched Into Orbit (50, 1:40), Stars in the Night (25, 1:80)Prices pending

    About five inserts a box, most of them Extraterrestrial Talent and the new Forces of the Diamond. At 1:6 and 1:8 packs these are $2 to $5 cards unless the player is Anthony or Ohtani.

  • Lunar box · Topps.com format: the same 80 cards with about 41 base, about 10 Lunar Eclipse /10 parallels and an autograph in three of four boxes (Cardsmiths Breaks)No eBay sale yet

    The format that puts the hits in every box: ten /10 parallels and most boxes with an autograph. Topps has not published a price and none has sold on eBay; if it lands near the hobby price it is the better box, and the one to compare before buying hobby.

What to skip

  • Paying $950 for the autograph. Most hobby boxes have none: one in about 2.7 boxes by Cardsmiths Breaks' averages, nearer one in five by Checklist Insider's pack odds. Buy it for the numbered colours or not at all.

  • Reading the presale price as the release price. 76 presale boxes sold at a $900 median and the last eight at $950, with Topps' own price $489.99; release week usually moves sealed prices one way or the other, and no 2026 single has sold yet to say which.

  • Buying the base rookies as an investment. Last year's Sasaki base RC is a $4 card a year on; Anthony's 2026 Topps base is $40 in PSA 10 after 158 sales. The base and the unnumbered Refractor are set-filler; the money is in the numbered colours.

  • Comparing the box to Topps Chrome. Cosmic Chrome is 80 cards and a hit in a minority of boxes; Chrome is 96 cards and an autograph a box for less. The reason to buy Cosmic is the parallel design holding value, not the hit rate.

  • Treating Nick Kurtz #136 as a rookie card. It carries no RC mark in this set; his 2025 Topps Chrome Update USC178 is the RC the market prices (PSA 10 $103 on the Raw-to-Gem board).

Singles buyers should wait for the first week of sales and buy the numbered Anthony and Murakami colours; box buyers should compare the Lunar box once Topps prices it.
