2026-27 Topps Flagship Basketball

6.4WAX SCORE / 10

Written Nov 3, two days before the hobby release (Nov 5). Topps sold the presale on Oct 6 at $169.99, $60 more than last year's $109.99, and eBay has it at $225: 170 single presale hobby boxes sold for a $225 median between Oct 7 and Nov 2, and the 34 sold in the last three days went for $227, so the price has not moved in a month. Jumbo boxes are $432 against $339.99 at Topps. Topps has not published the odds sheet, no single has a meaningful sale record yet (a few early-break inserts and one Golden Mirror SSP sold on Nov 2), so every single-card price below is pending; the structure, the class and last year's box are enough for a first verdict. This is the first pack-pulled NBA card of AJ Dybantsa, Darryn Peterson and Cameron Boozer, the three names the 2026 draft was about, and the second Topps flagship basketball set since the licence moved from Panini. The box is 240 cards for $225 with one autograph or relic, and a relic is the usual outcome; what carries the box is the base rookie run (#256 to #300) and the numbered colours of the top three, the way Flagg's #201 carried last year's. The case for it is last year's box: $119.99 on release day, $365 now, on a class that was one player deep. The case against is that this year's price already has that lesson in it, and a Black /75 or Gold /50 of a rookie outside the top three is a $10 card.

Tier 1 · The cards worth opening a hobby box for

  • AJ Dybantsa #256 RC · base and the numbered colours: Green /99, Black /75, Gold Rainbow /50, Orange /25, Black Rainbow /10, Red /5, FoilFractor 1/1Prices pending. Comp: Cooper Flagg 2025-26 Topps #201 base RC $105 in PSA 10 on the Raw-to-Gem board, $4 raw (Oct 11)

    The rookie of the set and the card every box is bought for. Last year's lesson is the base: Flagg's #201 is a $4 raw card that grades to $105, so the base rookie is a grading play, not a hold. The numbered colours under /99 are the cards with a market; the /10 and /5 come out of a case or less.

  • Darryn Peterson #257 and Cameron Boozer #258 RC · numbered coloursPrices pending. Comps: Dylan Harper 2025-26 Topps #202 base RC $118 in PSA 10, $1.73 raw; his Topps Chrome #252 Refractor $1,314 in PSA 10 (board, Oct 11 and 12)

    The second and third picks, who opened the season against each other on Oct 21 (Jazz at Grizzlies). Harper's cards show the shape: the paper base is worth almost nothing raw and $118 graded, the numbered chrome is where the money went. Peterson and Boozer colours will follow Dybantsa's at a discount that depends on their first month.

  • Rookie Photo Shoot Dual Autographs · Dybantsa with Peterson, Boozer with Wilson, ten cardsPrices pending

    The autograph the set will be remembered for if it holds up. Ten dual-signed cards; Topps has not said how many of each. One of these is a case-level hit at best and the one to buy the single of, not chase.

  • Flagship Real One Autographs · 86 cards, Gold /50, Orange /25, Black /10, Red /5, FoilFractor 1/1Prices pending

    The main autograph set, rookies on sticker for the most part. The hit is one autograph or relic per hobby box and the relic is the likelier of the two, so a Real One is not in every box; the rookie ones under /25 are the sales to watch.

Tier 2 · Mid-range: the numbered middle and the short prints

  • Numbered base parallels · Gold /2026, Yellow /399, Purple /250, Blue /150, Green /99, Black /75, Gold Rainbow /50, Orange /25, Wood /25; hobby-only Victory, jumbo-only Sandglitter Blue /500 to Red /5Prices pending

    Most of what a $225 box returns. Topps has not published odds, so how many numbered cards a box holds is unknown until the odds sheet lands; last year's hobby box averaged a few. The player decides the price: a Gold /2026 of a veteran is a $2 card, a Gold Rainbow /50 Dybantsa is not.

  • Super short prints · Bounce (25), All Kings (24, with a Gold 1/1 this year), Quest for the Crown (10)Prices pending

    The three SSP insert sets, each with Dybantsa (Bounce TB-16, All Kings AK-16), Peterson (TB-17, AK-17) and Boozer (TB-18). These were the biggest non-autograph sales in last year's set and will be again; the odds are unpublished.

  • Variations · Golden Mirror (300 cards), Clear /10 (100), Player Number (100, hobby-only), Team Color Border (100), Project Rookie (10), Break Point (10), LeBron 76ers SPA Giannis Golden Mirror sold for $499.99 on Nov 2 from an early break; one sale, not a price

    Five variation types where last year had fewer. Golden Mirror runs the whole set and is the one that will sell on the player; the Clear /10 is the rarest by print run. The LeBron 76ers SP is the first pack-pulled card of him in the jersey and a sale to watch on its own.

  • Rookie and class inserts · Class of 26 (20; Red /5, FoilFractor 1/1), Generation Now, No Limit, Rookie Code (40 each, Purple /250 to FoilFractor 1/1), Project RookiePrices pending

    The rookie inserts are where a second Dybantsa or Peterson card in the box comes from. Base versions are $2 to $10 cards; the numbered colours under /99 are the ones that trade.

Tier 3 · Everything else in the box

  • Base cards · the bulk of the 240, including the 45-card rookie run and the veterans (Giannis #17 as a Heat card, LeBron #57, Alex Sarr #1)Prices pending. Comp: Flagg 2025-26 Topps #201 base RC $4 raw, Harper #202 $1.73

    Most of the box. Outside Dybantsa, Peterson and Boozer the base rookies are dollar cards; the veterans are set-filler. 100 base cards also come as facsimile autographs, which are not autographs.

  • Common inserts · Poster Factory (20, new), Levitation (10, new), 1981-82 Topps, Hardwood Stars (25), Stars of NBA, Drivers, From the Vault, New School (40 each), The Daily Dribble, Comic Court (20 each)A Lendeborg Daily Dribble insert sold for $20 on Nov 2; one sale

    Several inserts a box. Poster Factory and Levitation are the new designs; 1981-82 Topps is the retro set for the design's 45th year and the one retail boxes are built around. $2 to $5 cards unless the player is Dybantsa.

  • Relics · Flagship Real One Relics (88 cards, Gold /50 to FoilFractor 1/1), Own the Game, Roundball RemnantsPrices pending

    The hit most hobby boxes will have. Plain jersey relics of veterans are $5 to $15 cards; a rookie patch under /25 is the exception.

What to skip

  • Paying $225 for the autograph. The guarantee is one autograph or relic, and in a flagship set the relic is the likelier card; buy the box for the base rookie run and the numbered colours or buy singles.

  • Reading last year's $365 box into this one. The 2025-26 box went from $119.99 to $365 on a one-player class with a $109.99 presale; this year's presale is $169.99 and the street price is already $225, so the first $100 of that move is priced in.

  • Holding the base rookies raw. Flagg's #201 is a $4 card raw and $105 in PSA 10 a year on; the paper base is a grading decision, not an investment, and only for the top three.

  • Buying numbered colours of rookies outside the top three at hobby prices. Caleb Wilson, Wagler and Brown are the next names; Harper's Topps paper base at $1.73 raw shows how fast the market narrows to the top of the class.

  • Mistaking Flagg, Harper, Knueppel and Edgecombe cards (#153 to #157) for rookies. They are second-year base cards without the RC mark; the 2025-26 cards are the ones the market prices.

  • The scores are a first read. Rookie class is scored on Dybantsa, Peterson and Boozer plus a 45-card run, a class with three names at the top where last year's had one. Hit quality is scored on one autograph or relic a box with the relic the likelier card. Print run is low: flagship is the widest-printed basketball product of the year, with hobby, jumbo, mega, blaster, hanger and Fanatics formats, and no run is published. Price vs. value uses $225 for 240 cards and one hit; last year's box tripling is the argument for, the $60 higher presale is the argument against. Pending after the first week: eBay sold medians with counts for the Dybantsa, Peterson and Boozer base and numbered colours, the SSP inserts, the Real One autographs and the dual autographs, and the odds sheet once Topps publishes it.

  • Verdict for now: the box to open this month if you want the three rookies in hand, at $225 and not more. The rookie class is the best case for a flagship box in years and the design held value last year; the hit is a relic more often than an autograph and the print run is large. Singles buyers should wait for the first week and buy numbered Dybantsa colours; set builders should buy the box; investors should compare last year's $365 box and remember that its $119.99 start is the part that cannot be repeated.
