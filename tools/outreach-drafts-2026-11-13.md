### r/basketballcards (Sat Nov 14)

**Title:** I ran the PSA grading math on 19 basketball cards: the 2025-26 Topps Chrome refractors, first-year 2012 Prizm, and some 90s and 00s Chrome. 15 clear the fee; the cheap rookies all lose.

The question I kept asking was "is this actually worth grading?", so I priced both outcomes for each card.

**Method.** All-in = raw price (eBay sold median) + PSA fee ($39.99 Value if the 10 is under $500, $59.99 Standard above) + $10 shipping. If it gems you sell the PSA 10 at 80% of its 90-day PSA median, minus a 13% selling fee. If it misses you sell a PSA 9 at 85% of its median, minus the fee. Weight the two by the card's real gem rate from the PSA pop report (10s / everything graded). "Expected profit" is what one attempt is worth on average. Only cards with 25+ PSA 10 sales in 90 days count, otherwise the 10 comp is a guess. Numbers are from Oct 12 to Nov 12.

What stood out:

- **The new Topps Chrome refractors have huge gaps.** Curry #201 Refractor is $78 raw and $790 in a 10. LeBron #127 is $68 and $820. Wemby #221 is $45 and $568. My guess is that a lot of the raw copies on eBay are ones that were already passed over for grading, so look hard at centring before you buy.
- **The 2012 Prizm base cards are a 9-covers-it trade.** Kobe #24 and LeBron #1 PSA 9s alone sell for more than the all-in. The risk is an 8, not a 9.
- **A high gem rate isn't the edge.** Zion #248 gems 54% and still loses $43 a card, because the 10 sells for $21 and the fee alone is $40.
- **The old Chrome cards carry a caveat.** On Duncan, Wade and Kobe Finest, 15% to 23% of the PSA pop is an 8 or lower. I treat every miss as a 9, which flatters those. With the sub-9s marked down they still clear, just by less.

Not advice, just arithmetic. Happy to run the numbers on any card people are thinking about sending in.
