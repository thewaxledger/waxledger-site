How the Raw-to-Gem Calculator decides

The question for every card is the same. If you buy it raw today and send it to PSA, what do you expect to clear once the slab comes back?

The math

  • All-in is the raw price plus the grading fee and shipping. That is the cost of one attempt.
  • Net on a 10 is the PSA 10 price, less the sell-date haircut and the selling fee. You sell months from now, after the pop has grown.
  • Net on a miss is the PSA 9 price times the miss share, less the selling fee. Most misses are 9s and some are 8s, so one blended number covers both.
  • Gem rate is PSA 10 pop divided by total pop, from the population report.
  • Expected profit is gem rate times net on a 10, plus the rest times net on a miss, minus all-in. ROI divides that by all-in.
  • Break-even gem rate is the gem rate at which you neither make nor lose money.
  • Safety margin is the gem rate minus the break-even, in points. This is the quality gate.

Why the PSA 9 price matters more than the multiple

A 10x raw-to-PSA-10 multiple looks great until you miss. When the 9 is worth less than the raw card plus the fee, every miss eats the whole attempt. When the 9 is worth more than you paid, a miss returns most of your money and a 25% gem rate can still be a strong grade. That is why the board ranks on return and safety margin, and ignores the multiple.

The verdict ladder

  1. Skip if the PSA 10 price is a modeled estimate with no real sales behind it.
  2. Fail if there are too few PSA 10 sales in 90 days. Thin comps are prices you may not get.
  3. Fail if expected profit is zero or negative.
  4. Verify pop if the card is profitable but running on a placeholder gem rate.
  5. Marginal if the safety margin is under the minimum.
  6. Grade otherwise, ranked by expected ROI.

Where the numbers come from

Prices are sold listings. Rows that have been checked against eBay sold listings say so in their source line, and rows that rest on a price guide alone are marked as not verified. Population counts come from the PSA pop report. Every card carries an as-of date.
